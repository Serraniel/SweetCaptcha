# Self-Hosted CAPTCHAs Stop Cheap Bots, Not Determined Agents

The project is doable, but "reliably block bots and AI agents" is not an honest goal; "stop commodity automation and today's default agents, and put a price on everything else" is. A self-hosted Docker stack can combine server-side decisions, cross-layer consistency checks, behavioural evidence, risk-scaled proof of work (PoW) and single-use tokens, and it can expose reCAPTCHA, hCaptcha and Turnstile-shaped APIs. Most real users would then pass silently. No independent evidence shows any open-source detector matching Turnstile or reCAPTCHA, and even commercial detectors let roughly half of purchased bot traffic through in the one in-the-wild study, from 2023. A coherent real browser on a residential IP with a humanised cursor will often pass, as will a human solver farm. Self-hosting also gives up cross-site history, which measurably drives silent passes, so first-visit decisions will be weaker than a global vendor's. The belief that only paid products offer true bot prevention is false as stated: free local detection exists, but mature cross-site reputation does stay paid or closed. Decisions must happen on the backend, and local scoring offers no way around the legal rules either, because a score sent to a server is still a derivation of device data. A strict privacy mode is definable and testable, but it is a cost and rate-limit mode, not a detection mode. "GDPR compliant" and "no consent needed" are not defensible product claims. This report is research, not legal advice; counsel should review any public compliance statement.

## "Reliable" holds only for the lower attacker tiers

The useful unit of analysis is the attacker tier, because a single "detection rate" hides everything that matters. The strongest in-the-wild measurement sent 507,080 purchased bot requests from 20 services at a honey site in 2023, and **about 45-53% evaded DataDome and BotD** ([FP-Inconsistent](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07647v3)). The paper is internally inconsistent about which figure belongs to which detector, so only the range is safe to cite. The table below is my judgement built from the evidence in later sections, and SweetCaptcha's own lab should replace it with measured numbers.

| Attacker tier | Expected outcome | What does the work | Durability |
|---|---|---|---|
| HTTP clients and crawlers that run no JavaScript | Mostly blocked | JS requirement, PoW, TLS and HTTP/2 mismatch | High |
| Stock Playwright, Puppeteer, Selenium | Mostly blocked | Driver artefacts, `navigator.webdriver` | Medium; trivially patched |
| JS-patched stealth tools | Partly blocked | Native-function tamper tests, Worker and iframe consistency | Medium; a maintenance race |
| Default LLM agents and agentic browsers | Mostly caught today | Teleporting clicks, burst typing or paste, sparse telemetry | Low; easy for agents to fix |
| Engine-patched browser, residential proxy, humanised input | Often passes | Plausibility, velocity, cost only | Structural limit |
| Agent in a real user's own browser | Often passes | Behaviour only | Structural limit |
| Human farm or paid solver | Passes the test itself | Velocity, device reuse, token-use mismatch | Structural limit |

The bottom three rows are the honest limit. Consistency checks catch sloppy lies, while a real browser on a real device driven by automation is coherent by definition. Local agents can also switch between human and automated control mid-session in the same browser ([Castle](https://blog.castle.io/from-detection-to-trust-the-evolving-challenge-of-ai-bot-authentication/)), so "human or bot" is sometimes the wrong question. Anything protected by this system still needs per-action limits and server-side abuse controls behind it.

The silent-pass goal also has a hard cost. In the one direct measurement, a real user's cookies and history cut mean reCAPTCHA v2 challenges **from 8.38 to 2.71**, after which bot and human challenge counts were statistically indistinguishable ([Breaking reCAPTCHAv2](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.08831v1)). That study used 50-100 runs per configuration on Google's demo site, so it shows direction rather than a transferable effect size. No vendor publishes how much of its detection comes from cross-site data, but Cloudflare's models also look for features shared by visitors who previously passed ([Cloudflare](https://blog.cloudflare.com/turnstile-private-captcha-alternative/)). A self-hosted deployment has no such history for a first-time visitor. It will therefore show more challenges, or pass more bots, on first contact.

## Contradictions and action structure carry detection; identity fingerprints do not

### Treat device signals as claims to cross-check, not as identifiers

Canvas, audio, WebGL, screen and hardware values are now legitimately noised or normalised by Brave ([Brave](https://brave.com/privacy-updates/4-fingerprinting-defenses-2.0/)), Firefox's resist-fingerprinting mode ([Mozilla](https://wiki.mozilla.org/Security/Fingerprinting)) and Mullvad and Tor ([Mullvad](https://mullvad.net/en/browser/hard-facts)). Secondary sources report that **Safari 26 noises canvas and audio read-back for all browsing by default** ([ppc.land](https://ppc.land/safari-26-tracking-changes-to-impact-marketing-measurement/)), though no primary WebKit document was found. "Canvas noise means suspicious" would therefore hit ordinary Apple users. Castle's guide to building detection in-house reaches the same design goal from the other side, prioritising resilience over uniqueness or stability, because the aim is detecting automation rather than tracking people ([Castle](https://blog.castle.io/roll-your-own-bot-detection-fingerprinting-javascript-part-1/)).

The best evidence for this approach is FP-Inconsistent. Rules needing no cross-site data, such as device claims against touch values or timezone against IP location, **cut evasion by about 45-48% and raised combined detection by 21-24 points to 71-77%** ([FP-Inconsistent](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07647v3)). Spatial rules within one request (70-76%) beat temporal rules across requests (48-57%), so the less invasive design also performed better. The cost is real: true negatives were 96.84% on 2,206 student requests, which means about 3% of those humans were flagged, mainly UA spoofers. All Tor requests were flagged through timezone-IP conflict. The data dates from 2023, predating both Safari 26 and current stealth tools, so these are templates rather than expected results.

The signal ranking below is my synthesis, since no source ranks value against privacy cost. Worker and iframe comparisons rest partly on the open-source [rebrowser-bot-detector](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rebrowser/rebrowser-bot-detector/main/README.md), which rates UA, platform and brand mismatch in a Worker as strong and languages and core count as weaker. Native-function lie tests are documented in [CreepJS](https://github.com/abrahamjuliot/creepjs/blob/master/src/lies/index.ts), whose code is MIT but whose name forks must not reuse.

| Priority | Signal | Why | Handling |
|---|---|---|---|
| Build first | Worker and iframe consistency; JS engine against claimed browser; UA against UA-CH against headers | Cheap, low entropy, compares rather than identifies | Compare keyed hashes; store rule IDs only |
| Build first | Driver artefacts and `webdriver === true` | Catches stock tools | Positive evidence only; `false` means nothing; match structurally, as [BotD](https://github.com/fingerprintjs/BotD/blob/main/src/sources/distinctive_properties.ts) does |
| Build first | Native-function tamper checks | Catches JS patching; blind to engine patches | Weigh automation lies heavily; discount known extension patterns |
| Build next | JA4 and HTTP/2 against claimed browser family | Strong against HTTP clients; identifies a client type, not a person ([Cloudflare](https://developers.cloudflare.com/bots/additional-configurations/ja3-ja4-fingerprint/)) | Needs TLS termination you control; mark absent rather than suspicious |
| Build next | Local ASN class, cloud ranges, Tor list | Context only; no third party | Datacentre is medium weight; Tor routes to a privacy-compatible flow, never a block |
| Soft only | WebGL renderer class and limits, screen and pointer plausibility, timezone against IP country | Catches impossible devices; VDI and VMs cause false positives | Bucket values; **exempt UTC**, which alone addresses the Tor false positive |
| Avoid | Canvas or audio hash as identity, full font lists, device labels, high-entropy UA-CH, long-term linking | Highest privacy cost, declining value | Canvas as a noise-status boolean at most |
| Retire | Own-`stack` getter CDP probe | **V8 commits of 7 and 9 May 2025 killed it** ([Castle](https://blog.castle.io/why-a-classic-cdp-bot-detection-signal-suddenly-stopped-working-and-nobody-noticed/)) | Remove; variants on `Error.prototype.name` are contested ([Scrapfly](https://scrapfly.io/blog/posts/chrome-cdp-stealth-browser-automation-detection-explained)) and flag DevTools users, so log only |
| Lab only | Popup and `history.replaceState` flood probes | Reveal launch flags | Unacceptable behaviour inside a widget |

Three facts shape expectations. Since the 2022 headless unification, API-discrepancy detection has become largely ineffective ([Castle](https://blog.castle.io/from-puppeteer-stealth-to-nodriver-how-anti-detect-frameworks-evolved-to-evade-bot-detection/)). BotD's own table says it does not detect puppeteer-extra stealth, browserless or undetected-chromedriver, and its roadmap is stability-only ([BotD](https://github.com/fingerprintjs/BotD)). Camoufox patches Firefox in C++, so JavaScript lie checks are unlikely to fire ([Camoufox](https://github.com/daijro/camoufox)); benchmarks of it conflict and none is independent ([Centinel](https://www.centinelanalytica.com/blog/what-is-camoufox), [DEV](https://dev.to/ianlpaterson/anti-detect-browser-benchmark-2026-7-stealth-tools-31-cloudflare-targets-651-verdicts-4361)). A continuously updated lab of current browsers and stealth tools is therefore part of the product rather than a test convenience.

Network fingerprinting carries a licensing trap. **JA4 itself is BSD, but JA4H, JA4T, JA4L and the rest are under FoxIO License 1.1**, which excludes hosted or managed service provision and paid support, and FoxIO describes them as patent-pending ([FoxIO](https://github.com/FoxIO-LLC/ja4), [licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FoxIO-LLC/ja4/main/LICENSE)). Ship JA4 and the published Akamai-style HTTP/2 format ([white paper](https://www.blackhat.com/docs/eu-17/materials/eu-17-Shuster-Passive-Fingerprinting-Of-HTTP2-Clients-wp.pdf)) by default, and have counsel review anything else before commercial distribution. The status of any Akamai-method patent was not established. Corporate TLS inspection will also produce mismatches between TLS and browser, at a rate no source quantified, so this evidence must never block alone.

### Behaviour works today because agents barely move, and that advantage will expire

Lab kinematics look impressive. BeCAPTCHA-Mouse reports 93-99% from one trajectory against synthetic paths, in a controlled task sampled at 200 Hz ([BeCAPTCHA-Mouse](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2005.00890)). Real agents fail much earlier. Akamai's telemetry on Comet, Atlas and the Claude Chrome extension found **63.2% of agentic requests had zero mouse events, 35.8% were below its evaluation minimum and only 1.0% were evaluable**, so its mouse model flagged under 1% because it had too little data to run ([Akamai](https://www.akamai.com/blog/security-research/identifying-agentic-automation-behavioral-telemetry)). "Not enough data" must feed the score rather than pass silently, and "a click with no approach path" is currently a better signal than any kinematic feature. FP-Agent saw the same pattern across seven agents, along with distinctive typing: Browser Use at 5.31 ms between keys, Manus at about 1.39 ms, and paste or change events elsewhere ([FP-Agent](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.01247v1)).

The headline FP-Agent result, **behavioural F1 of 0.9994 and browser fingerprint F1 of 0.8223**, should be read with its limits. It is closed-world, the agents did not try to evade, and on a held-out task behavioural F1 fell to about 0.63-0.80 while the combined model held at 0.93-0.97 ([FP-Agent](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.01247v1)). This argues for combining layers, and for caution about behaviour in unfamiliar task shapes. Behavioural F1 also plateaued only after about three minutes, far longer than a login form allows, so widget-scale accuracy will be lower. I found no independent replication of any 2025-2026 agent figure.

The teleport signal is perishable. ghost-cursor already provides Bézier paths, Fitts's-law point counts and overshoot ([ghost-cursor](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Xetera/ghost-cursor/master/README.md)), and agent frameworks could adopt that with little effort. I found no measurement of its defeat rate against any published detector. In reCAPTCHA v2's outcomes, Bézier and straight paths made no difference, although no movement at all did worse ([Breaking reCAPTCHAv2](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.08831v1)). `isTrusted` is weak as well, since CDP-dispatched input reports true ([Clearcote](https://www.clearcotelabs.com/audit/checks/event-istrusted-provenance)). Timing is similarly exposed: in an Oxford study of model attribution, injected 5 s delays cut unadapted macro-F1 from about 0.8 to 0.2-0.3, though retraining on action structure recovered about 0.67-0.7 ([arXiv 2605.14786](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.14786)). Keep structural features alongside timing, and have weights fall automatically when the lab shows a signal decaying.

Base rates decide what these scores may do. Akamai's sequence model reached ROC-AUC 0.981 but **PR-AUC of only 0.661 at about 85 human sessions per agent session**, and Akamai labels it proof of concept. My arithmetic: at that ratio, a 1% false-positive rate wrongly flags about 0.85 humans per agent session, so even perfect recall gives roughly 54% precision. Behaviour therefore cannot justify a denial. It can justify escalation, and it should do so only when combined with other evidence.

Accessibility sharpens this. Screen readers can send synthetic mouse events, and keyboard activation under NVDA reports `detail` of 0 ([Angular CDK](https://app.unpkg.com/@angular/cdk@21.2.4/files/fesm2022/_fake-event-detection-chunk.mjs.map)), which overlaps with the agent signature. **I found no measured false-positive rates for keyboard-only, screen-reader, switch, eye-tracking or tremor users on any behavioural classifier.** This is the largest evidence gap in the design, and SweetCaptcha will need its own testing with disabled users. Input modes need separate models, and absent data needs three distinct states: not applicable (a touch device has no hover), abstained (too few samples or blocked events), and contradictory absence (a mouse pointer type and a click with no moves). Only the third counts as evidence.

### Per-site calibration needs self-generated positives

Purely unsupervised detection does not work at realistic base rates. On identical data Akamai measured PR-AUC of 0.044 for a one-class SVM, 0.038 for Isolation Forest and 0.410 for a VAE, against 0.661 for a supervised model ([Akamai](https://www.akamai.com/blog/security-research/identifying-agentic-automation-behavioral-telemetry)). Random Forest on hand-built features competes with recurrent models on small data ([BeCAPTCHA-Mouse](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2005.00890)). The way out requires no third-party data: run Browser Use, Playwright, CDP and Bézier scripts against your own widget to generate labels, and keep that harness as a regression suite. Then run in shadow mode to learn per-site human distributions by input mode.

The scoring design I propose, which no published study validates, sums capped log-likelihood ratios with correlated signals grouped so that one root cause counts once. It outputs reason codes and three bands: allow, escalate, deny. Deny requires hard evidence plus rate or reputation evidence, and never rests on kinematics alone. Operators would set a target human challenge rate of perhaps 1-2%, with band thresholds derived from it. Baselines should include only sessions with no hard signals, with contribution capped per IP and ASN, since attackers can otherwise poison them through slow drift. Very low-traffic sites should fall back to shipped priors, and the sample sizes needed are unknown. Castle's advice for a decoupled policy layer with dry runs and staged rollout fits this ([Castle](https://blog.castle.io/roll-your-own-bot-detection-server-side-detection-part-2/)).

## Proof of work prices actions, and manual challenges are an expiring fallback

### PoW is a rate limiter that needs single-use tokens

PoW filters clients that run no JavaScript and charges for each action; it does not detect humans. Cap and FCaptcha both concede the limit: scripts can solve it without a browser, and compute cost will not price out an attacker ([Cap](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tiagozip/cap/main/docs/guide/workings.md), [FCaptcha](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WebDecoy/FCaptcha/main/README.md)). The Anubis debate provides field detail. At the default difficulty, Tavis Ormandy estimated about 2^16 SHA-256 operations for 7 days of access ([Ormandy](https://lock.cmpxchg8b.com/anubis.html)), and Farid Zakaria measured about 1.3 ms in native Go against about 130 ms in browser JS, with scrapers caching the cookie and paying once ([Zakaria](https://fzakaria.com/2026/07/09/who-does-anubis-actually-stop)). Anubis's author reportedly describes it as mostly rate limiting ([RuntimeWire](https://runtimewire.com/article/anubis-bypass-proof-of-work-bot-wall-limits)), and that framing is the right one. Operators report results, such as Duke's 4M blocked requests a day, but with no control group and with some real users blocked ([LWN](https://lwn.net/Articles/1028558)). The 2004 objection also stands: setting the cost high enough to matter hurts legitimate senders, and botnets pay nothing for stolen compute ([Laurie and Clayton](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/~rnc1/proofwork.pdf)).

The most actionable lesson is that **token lifetime matters as much as difficulty**. A 7-day reusable cookie lets an attacker pay once, whereas a single-use token charges per protected action. Algorithm choice matters against GPUs but not against botnets: one unreplicated measurement shows SHA-256 with a GPU advantage of about 150x and HashWX about 2x ([Cap HashWX](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tiagozip/cap/main/docs/guide/hashwx.md)). The HashWX figure comes from an unpublished implementation, and a purpose-built one could change it. Memory-hard Argon2id offers another route but risks failure on low-memory phones, as ALTCHA's `retryOnOutOfMemoryError` option suggests ([ALTCHA](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/altcha-org/altcha/main/README.md)).

Phones are the binding constraint on "almost automatic". At Cap's default HashWX difficulty, **median solve time runs from about 490 ms on an M3 desktop and 1.1 s on a Galaxy S24 to 5.9 s on a Vivo Y21, with 11 s the slowest** ([Cap HashWX](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tiagozip/cap/main/docs/guide/hashwx.md)). The reported HashWX rate implies about 10,000 tokens per hour on one 16-thread desktop CPU at 1M hashes per token (my arithmetic), which is minor against scrapers but meaningful for single-use form spam. Raising difficulty hits the weakest human device first. So keep the default low, raise it only on risk evidence as W3C describes ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/TR/turingtest/)), cap it, and show progress on long solves. My recommendation is to start solving when the user begins interacting with the protected form, so the work is usually finished by submission. FCaptcha's approach of scoring solutions under 1.5 s as "too fast" illustrates a further use as a timing gate, but it conflicts with strict privacy mode if timing informs a decision.

### Challenges are an escalation step with a shrinking margin

Recognition and grid puzzles should not be a security boundary. Fine-tuned YOLOv8 reached a 100% solve rate on reCAPTCHA v2's demo site, with only 82.4% classifier accuracy, because retries were free ([Breaking reCAPTCHAv2](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.08831v1)). In a 1,400-person study, bots scored 85-100% against humans at 50-85% ([Searles et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.12108)), so a challenge is itself a false-positive source. COGNITION puts many task types under 10 cents per solve with raw model calls, and notes human farms run at about 0.5-2 cents per challenge with 80-90% accuracy ([COGNITION](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/usenixsecurity26-wang-junyu.pdf)). Solver-review pages list about $1.20 per 1,000 Turnstile tokens, a self-reported, snippet-level figure that no one has independently measured ([AIMultiple](https://research.aimultiple.com/captcha-solving-services), [Scrapfly](https://scrapfly.io/blog/posts/best-captcha-solving-api)). A challenge is therefore a small per-action cost plus a signal, and it cannot deter an attacker whose per-action profit exceeds that cost.

Interactive tasks hold up better in published benchmarks, all run by their own authors with small or unstated human samples and no disabled participants.

| Challenge study | Best machine | Humans |
|---|---|---|
| [Halligan](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/teoh), generalised visual solver | 60.7%; 70.6% on unseen in-the-wild | Not stated in abstract |
| [Open CaptchaWorld](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.24878), interactive | 40.0% | 93.3% |
| [Spatial CAPTCHA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.03863) | 31.0% first attempt | Figure not retrieved |
| [Next-Gen CAPTCHAs](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.09012v1), 27 families | 5.9% (family peaks up to 60%) | 98.8%, about 31 s each |
| [IllusionCAPTCHA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05461) | About 0% | 86.95% first attempt |

The margins are fragile. Next-Gen's costs are revealing: at the reported totals, assuming even division across puzzles, GPT-5.2 costs about $390 per success while Gemini-3 Flash costs about $0.39 (my arithmetic), so the cheap fast model is the realistic economic threat ([Next-Gen](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.09012v1)). Attackers also need not use an LLM at all, since combining models with classical vision reportedly exceeded 90% across one set of darknet CAPTCHA types, a preprint I know only by snippet ([arXiv 2608.28794](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.28794v1)). COGNITION's hardening test, replacing grids with "click all visible eyes" at 15 px tolerance, dropped several models to 0% but left Claude Sonnet 4.5 at 23.3% and Qwen3-VL at 20.0%, and it covers only direct API calls without agent orchestration. Expect a window of months to a few years (my judgement), and rotate families using observed pass data, watching for solve-time convergence and success spikes as COGNITION recommends. Free retries must also be capped.

Accessibility limits what this can achieve. WCAG 2.2 SC 3.3.8 bars cognitive function tests in authentication without an alternative, and logic puzzles are not excepted, nor can transcribing audio serve as the alternative ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/accessible-authentication.html)). Whether a human check counts as "authentication" is disputed ([W3C list](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-apa/2022Jan/0002.html)), and vendor claims that the law bans CAPTCHAs overreach. **Hard time pressure is a poor primary design**, because SC 2.2.1 requires a limit to be removable or extendable tenfold ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/timing-adjustable.html)). No study measures a timed reactive challenge against both agents and disabled users. Treat latency as a scored signal rather than a limit. Drag and path tasks also need keyboard and single-pointer equivalents ([SC 2.1.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/keyboard.html), [SC 2.5.7](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/dragging-movements.html)), and those equivalents are likely easier for agents, since agents already have type and click tools. So the families most resistant to agents are the least accessible, and accessible versions lose much of the gain.

The escalation ladder should therefore run: silent pass, low-cost PoW with probes, higher PoW, then a choice between an interactive challenge and a non-puzzle alternative. That alternative can be an emailed code, a passkey for known users, or a staffed contact route. It must itself be rate-limited. A no-JavaScript route is needed too, since PoW requires JS. The EU Accessibility Act has applied to consumer e-commerce since 28 June 2025, with an exemption for microenterprises ([Directive 2019/882](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32019L0882)). I found no CAPTCHA-specific enforcement, and SC 3.3.8 might not yet sit in the presumption-of-conformity baseline ([EqualWeb](https://www.equalweb.com/platform/standards/en-301549.html), a vendor source). Building to WCAG 2.2 AA is still the cheap choice for a non-interactive default.

## "All true bot prevention is paid" is half right: cross-site data is the real paywall

As stated, the belief is false. MIT-licensed FCaptcha claims 40+ behavioural and environmental signals with JA3/JA4 and no phone-home, Apache-licensed Cap adds per-challenge browser instrumentation on top of PoW, and MIT-form go-away offers TLS fingerprinting and rules for reverse-proxy gating. Turnstile's free plan has unlimited verification, though it is hosted and closed ([plans](https://developers.cloudflare.com/turnstile/plans/)). The narrower version does hold: **no self-hosted product reviewed offers mature cross-site reputation and ML intelligence without paying or accepting closed code**. Local signals can be open-sourced, whereas reputation at scale depends on traffic a single self-hoster never sees.

| Product | Licence | Self-hosted detection | Catch |
|---|---|---|---|
| [FCaptcha](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WebDecoy/FCaptcha/main/README.md) | MIT | Behavioural, headless and CDP, JA3/JA4, Web Bot Auth; PoW as timing gate | About 11 months old, single vendor, efficacy untested independently |
| [Cap](https://capjs.js.org/guide/instrumentation) | [Apache-2.0](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tiagozip/cap/main/LICENSE) | HashWX PoW plus instrumentation | Marketing says "Turnstile-tier" but its [technical page](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tiagozip/cap/main/docs/guide/workings.md) says it does not decide humanity |
| [ALTCHA](https://github.com/altcha-org/altcha) | MIT | PoW only | Risk scoring is in [Sentinel](https://altcha.org/docs/sentinel/), proprietary, from [€99/month](https://altcha.org/pricing/) |
| [Anubis](https://github.com/TecharoHQ/anubis) | MIT | PoW, policy rules, GeoIP | Page gate rather than form CAPTCHA; advanced fingerprinting [reserved for paid BotStopper](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/TecharoHQ/anubis/main/docs/docs/admin/botstopper.mdx) |
| [go-away](https://git.gammaspectra.live/git/go-away/raw/branch/master/README.md) | MIT-form | CEL rules, JA3N/JA4, network filters | Reverse proxy, small project |
| [mCaptcha](https://github.com/mCaptcha/mCaptcha) | AGPL-3.0 | SHA-256 PoW | Last commit October 2025 |
| [Procaptcha](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/prosopo/captcha/main/SELF_HOSTING.md) | Apache-2.0 except detection | Fixed challenge types | Self-hosting **skips bot detection**, the only closed part |
| [Private Captcha](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PrivateCaptcha/self-hosting/main/README.md) | [PolyForm Noncommercial](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PrivateCaptcha/PrivateCaptcha/main/LICENSE) | PoW with scaling | Commercial use needs a paid licence |
| [Friendly Captcha](https://developer.friendlycaptcha.com/docs/v2/guides/self-hosted-endpoint) | Widget open, service proprietary | None | "Self-Hosted Endpoint" only forwards to their API |

The paid side shows what SweetCaptcha cannot easily copy. All commercial systems share one shape: passive telemetry, a rotating obfuscated collector, a model score, then a graduated response. Cloudflare confirms its pipeline includes PoW, proof of space, web API probing and browser-quirk checks from a rotating suite, without publishing parameters ([Cloudflare](https://blog.cloudflare.com/turnstile-private-captcha-alternative/)). It also reported a 91% fall in its own CAPTCHA use with Managed Challenge, a 2022 vendor figure ([Intelligent CIO](https://www.intelligentcio.com/me/2022/10/07/cloudflare-launches-a-user-friendly-privacy-preserving-alternative-to-captchas/)). Arkose exposes separate global and per-customer scores and a first-class fraud-farm category, showing both kinds of signal matter in production ([Arkose](https://developer.arkoselabs.com/docs/risk-score)). Reverse-engineering suggests reCAPTCHA's VM and Kasada's reported design make signals expensive to forge per version rather than hiding a permanent secret ([RESONEO](https://think.resoneo.com/botguard-google/), [InsideReCaptcha](https://github.com/toogle/InsideReCaptcha)), though the Kasada, Akamai and HUMAN details rest largely on bypass vendors. **No independent head-to-head of any of these exists in the sources**, so competitor rankings cannot be supported, and published vendor comparisons should not be repeated.

## All decisions belong on the backend, and client scoring buys no legal shortcut

Client-side processing cannot be trusted for security. Whoever controls the client can skip the scoring and post any verdict, and a signing key shipped to the client is public. Cloudflare states the base case plainly: the widget alone does not protect your forms, and server-side validation is mandatory on every submission ([Turnstile](https://developers.cloudflare.com/turnstile/get-started/server-side-validation/)). The client can usefully do three things: pay a cost the server verifies, reduce raw signals before sending (a privacy benefit, not a security one), and supply weak evidence the server cross-checks.

Client scoring fails on the legal side as well. The EDPB holds that processing inside the browser is not access only "as long as the information does not leave the device", and that once a derivation is accessed, Art. 5(3) applies; locally produced results sent to a server remain in scope ([EDPB ¶¶44, 53](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2024-10/edpb_guidelines_202302_technical_scope_art_53_eprivacydirective_v2_en_0.pdf)). **So a purely local verdict that stays on the device gives the server nothing, and one that reaches the server is both forgeable and still regulated.** Sending coarse features rather than raw values remains worthwhile, since it is good minimisation under the GDPR.

| Concern | Client may | Server must |
|---|---|---|
| Verdict or score | Show UX state only | Compute from server-held policy |
| PoW | Solve | Issue random signed challenge, set difficulty, verify, consume once |
| Features | Reduce to coarse values | Cross-check against headers and TLS; assume fabrication |
| Timing and behaviour | Report as a weak hint | Measure issue-to-redeem time; distrust client clocks |
| Integrity | Obfuscate, as delay only | Randomise probes, detect impossible combinations, enforce rate limits |
| Secrets | Hold none | Hold signing keys, siteverify secrets, salts |
| Tokens | Carry opaque tokens | Issue, validate, bind, consume once |

The protocol design below is engineering judgement built on published security guidance. Issue a signed challenge carrying site, action, origin, difficulty and expiry, then issue a separate pass token after verification, using different keys so the two cannot be confused, per [RFC 8725](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc8725). HMAC-SHA-256 with per-site keys suits the normal case where issuer and verifier are one service; Ed25519 suits separate verifiers. Opaque random tokens are also valid and simplify minimisation. A signature cannot stop replay, as PASETO notes ([PASETO](https://github.com/paseto-standard/paseto-spec)), so use an atomic check-and-set such as Redis `SET NX EX`, with a 2-5 minute TTL. Turnstile's 300 s, single-use model is the benchmark. Fail closed on logins and allow configurable degradation on low-risk forms. Support an idempotency key so a retried verify does not spuriously fail.

Replay of real human trajectories is an established threat ([ReMouse](https://doaj.org/article/7ad2dc9734db4662afd4ec66e5040055)). Server-chosen random target positions make replayed paths miss, and storing coarse trajectory sketches briefly lets the server flag near-duplicates across different nonces. Chained batch hashes only force forgers to regenerate data. None of these has a peer-reviewed measurement. Origin and hostname checks stop other sites' browsers from misusing a site key, but non-browser bots can spoof them, and hCaptcha itself warns its `hostname` comes from the browser ([hCaptcha](https://docs.hcaptcha.com/)).

An open-source project must follow Kerckhoffs: assume feature lists and weights are public, and keep keys, per-site distributions and challenge content secret. Obfuscation buys delay only. One author reports decrypting the Turnstile program on ChatGPT, which I know only via a search summary of a single-author write-up ([buchodi mirror](https://hn.jakechampion.name/reader/https://www.buchodi.com/chatgpt-wont-let-you-type-until-cloudflare-reads-your-react-state-i-decrypted-the-program-that-does-it/)). Small deployments also lack the fleet telemetry that makes rotation worthwhile. An opaque VM additionally conflicts with the admin's duty to describe the processing accurately, so ship a small stable loader with Subresource Integrity ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Security/Defenses/Subresource_Integrity)), deliver rotating probes as auditable data, and keep obfuscation off in strict mode.

Hardware attestation is no shortcut. Private Access Tokens validate a device and signed application rather than behaviour, and need an external attester and issuer ([Cloudflare](https://blog.cloudflare.com/eliminating-captchas-on-iphones-and-macs-using-new-standard/)); whether a self-hosted operator can participate was not established. WebAuthn attestation shows authenticator origin rather than humanity ([WebAuthn](https://www.w3.org/TR/webauthn-3/)). Web Environment Integrity was removed after heavy criticism ([OSnews](https://www.osnews.com/story/137723/google-drops-web-environment-integrity-proposal/)), so requiring attested environments is a poor direction for an open project. Web Bot Auth deserves support, but as a separate policy class: it tells you who is signing, not why, and its IETF charter excludes detecting non-participants ([Cloudflare](https://developers.cloudflare.com/bots/reference/bot-verification/web-bot-auth), [IETF](https://datatracker.ietf.org/wg/webbotauth/about/)). A valid signature should not count as human, and a missing one should not count as bot.

## Privacy claims must describe the code, because the law is unsettled

### Reading device data triggers consent rules unless a narrow, contested exemption applies

Art. 5(3) of the ePrivacy Directive applies when JavaScript reads device properties and sends them, whether or not the data is personal and whether or not the operator is first-party ([EDPB ¶¶33-34](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2024-10/edpb_guidelines_202302_technical_scope_art_53_eprivacydirective_v2_en_0.pdf), [Planet49](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:62017CJ0673)). The EDPB expressly does not decide exemptions (¶¶4, 40), so neither "EDPB cleared it" nor "EDPB banned it" is accurate. The closest EU text is WP29 Opinion 9/2014: the user-centric security exemption can cover fingerprinting, but not for services the user did not explicitly request, and never with secondary use ([WP224](https://ec.europa.eu/justice/article-29/documentation/opinion-recommendation/files/2014/wp224_en.pdf)).

National positions pull in different directions. **CNIL's FAQ (dated 29 April 2026) is the most on-point statement**: no consent is needed where the sole purpose is securing the site or an authentication mechanism for users, while tools that also serve other purposes, reCAPTCHA named, do need it ([CNIL](https://www.cnil.fr/fr/cookies-et-autres-traceurs/regles/cookies/FAQ)). Its Cityscoot decision rejected reCAPTCHA precisely because it allowed analysis by Google ([SAN-2023-003 ¶86](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2023-08/fr_sa_2023-03-decisionpublic.pdf)). Against that, law-firm reporting quotes a July 2026 CNIL FAQ on email pixels saying the exemption "does not cover fraud prevention in general (e.g., bot detection)" ([Norton Rose Fulbright](https://connections.nortonrosefulbright.com/post/102o01q/tracking-pixels-in-emails-a-cnil-recommendation-to-clarify-practices-and-secure)), which I read second-hand only.

Germany's DSK guidance, version 1.2 of November 2024, treats reading properties by JavaScript to build a fingerprint as access (Rn. 24). It accepts that user-oriented fraud prevention and IT security can belong to the base service (Rn. 74) and that login protection can be strictly necessary (Rn. 83), but it rejects a blanket "security" label, and additional functions count as wished only when actually used, such as filling in a form (Rn. 76). It also leaves no room for balancing: a legitimate-interest assessment cannot establish the § 25 TDDDG exemption (Rn. 68-69) ([DSK](https://datenschutzkonferenz-online.de/media/oh/OH_Digitale_Dienste.pdf)). Version 1.1's carve-out for data inevitably sent on page load, such as User-Agent, appears to have been dropped ([v1.1](https://www.datenschutz.bremen.de/sixcms/media.php/13/20221205_oh_Telemedien_2021_Version_1_1_Vorlage_104_DSK_final.pdf)).

**The only court ruling found on point is hostile.** Austria's BVwG (W298 2274626-1, 13 September 2024; not LG Wien, as sometimes assumed) held that reCAPTCHA was "technically not necessary" for operating the website, so consent was required ([RIS](https://www.ris.bka.gv.at/Dokument.wxe?Abfrage=Bvwg&Dokumentnummer=BVWGT_20240913_W298_2274626_1_00)). Its facts differ: Google, a party website, a page-load cookie, no protected action. Yet that reasoning is not limited to third parties and could reach a self-hosted tool. Self-hosting therefore removes the ground CNIL relied on, but not the ground the Austrian court relied on. I found no German court ruling on CAPTCHAs, no hCaptcha decision, and nothing on self-hosted CAPTCHAs at all; whether the Austrian ruling is final was not established.

The UK is friendliest. PECR Schedule A1 para 4(2) now names preventing or detecting fraud as a strictly necessary purpose ([DUAA Sch. 12](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/18/schedule/12)), although spam may not be "fraud", and I could read the ICO's guidance only second-hand. In the EU, the Digital Omnibus is **not law**. The Council's October 2026 mandate would exempt access "solely related to and strictly necessary for" maintaining the "technical security" of the means strictly necessary for a requested service, with 24 months to transpose, and it is unclear whether spam or scalping qualifies ([ST 14756/26](https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/ST-14756-2026-INIT/en/pdf)). No trilogue had begun as of late October ([tracker](https://eudigitalpolicy.eu/instruments/data-omnibus/)), so marketing should not rely on it.

Exemption strength falls by use case (my grading from these sources): login and credential defence is strongest, then password reset and account creation, then contact and comment forms, which are contested, with site-wide bot scoring and scalping weakest. Consent is no fallback for detection, as CNIL notes, because refusal would let bots bypass the tool. If a visitor refuses, the honest fallback is the non-reading path.

The GDPR layer is separate. A dynamic IP is personal data for a site with legal means of identification, and that site may have a legitimate interest in operability ([Breyer](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:62014CJ0582)). Recitals [47](https://gdpr-info.eu/recitals/no-47/) and [49](https://gdpr-info.eu/recitals/no-49/) support fraud prevention and security "to the extent strictly necessary". Hashing does not help: DSK treats a hash as a form of fingerprint (Rn. 23), and SRB's relative test does not favour an operator holding the key and logs ([SRB summary](https://www.datenschutz-notizen.de/pseudonymised-data-not-always-personal-according-to-the-latest-cjeu-judgement-1555813/)). "Pseudonymised" is the honest word. Whether a hard block on an automated score engages Art. 22 was not researched, and an alternative path reduces that exposure.

### Three named modes keep the tradeoff visible

There is one real tension here. The strongest legal posture collects the least and escalates on risk; if PoW and rate limits are adequate, active fingerprinting is hard to call "strictly" necessary. The strongest detection collects on every protected action, because the signals that catch agents are the same reads that need justifying. I would resolve this per form, running device reads only on user-initiated protected actions and never on page load, and reserving higher-entropy reads for server-triggered escalation.

| Mode | What runs | Art. 5(3) position | Detection power |
|---|---|---|---|
| **True privacy** (opt-in) | PoW and transient server rate limits only | Strongest position; not proven outside scope | None against browsers; cost only |
| Local-assist | Coarse features computed locally, minimal derivation sent | Applies; exemption reasoning required | Weak, forgeable evidence |
| Standard or escalation | Server-scored signals on protected actions, higher-entropy reads on risk | Applies; analysis and notice required | Highest available locally |

The name "true privacy" should attach only to a mode defined by what the code does not do, with each guarantee testable by an admin. The guarantees below extend the notes' proposal, and the verification column is my suggestion.

| Guarantee | Verifiable by |
|---|---|
| No script reads of device or browser properties for detection | Source audit, instrumented API test |
| No cookies, localStorage or IndexedDB | Browser storage inspection |
| No persistent or per-device identifier | Token and log inspection |
| No mouse, keystroke or timing telemetry | Network capture of the payload |
| No third-party requests; all assets self-hosted | Network capture |
| Only upload is the PoW solution to a server-issued challenge, plus what HTTP inherently carries | Network capture |
| IP and headers used transiently for rate limits, never persisted, with no fingerprint from headers or TLS | Config manifest, data store inspection |
| Any setting that breaks these visibly exits the mode | Admin UI, machine-readable manifest |

Two details need care. First, the argument that a PoW solution is not "information stored on the device" is my own and untested, and it weakens if solve time, thread count or hardware values are reported. For that reason strict mode should not score solve time, even as measured by the server. Second, a reCAPTCHA v3 integrator expects a score, and Contact Form 7 defaults to a 0.50 threshold ([CF7](https://plugins.svn.wordpress.org/contact-form-7/trunk/modules/recaptcha/service.php)). Strict mode has no real score to give. My recommendation is a documented fixed value that clears common thresholds, plus an extension field stating the mode, so that forms keep working and integrators can see that the number carries no humanity information. The fallback must be documented as well, since an email route collects personal data.

Strict mode must state its own weakness in the product itself: it raises costs and does not detect sophisticated bots or distinguish humans. That honesty is what makes the name defensible.

### Claims should be verifiable technical facts

Compliance attaches to the controller's whole processing, and CNIL places the checking on deployers. Every statement should be testable through a published signal register per mode, a network self-test and open source. Exposure under unfair-competition and deception law (UWG, UCPD, FTC Act) is the likely enforcement route for overclaiming, but it was not researched and counsel should confirm it. California pen-register suits over fingerprinting add US risk, with $2,500 per violation claimed and defences contested ([K&L Gates](https://www.klgates.com/thought-leadership/Pen-Register-and-Trap-and-Trace-Claims-The-Latest-Wave-of-CIPA-Litigation-3-4-2024)). CCPA treatment remains a gap.

| Avoid | Why | Say instead, only if true for that configuration |
|---|---|---|
| "GDPR compliant", "DSGVO-konform" | Attaches to the controller, not the tool | "Designed to support data minimisation. You remain the controller." |
| "No consent needed", "no cookie banner" | Contested; contrary to BVwG reasoning | "Strict mode sets no cookies and reads no device fingerprint. Whether consent is required depends on your configuration and national law." |
| "Anonymous", "no personal data" | IP is often personal; hashes are pseudonymous | "Does not store IP addresses by default. Signals in [mode] are pseudonymised with a rotating per-site key and deleted after [X]." |
| "No tracking" | Overbroad if any linkage exists | "No cross-site identifiers, no third-party sharing, no reuse for analytics, in the software as shipped." |
| "Approved by CNIL/DSK" | No such approval found | "Defaults take CNIL FAQ Q17 and DSK OH Digitale Dienste into account. This is not regulator approval." |
| "Nothing leaves your browser" | False if any result is sent | "Raw signals stay in the browser; only [named coarse features] go to your own server." |
| "Bot-proof", "Turnstile-grade" | Unsupported | "Raises attacker cost; verified on your server. Measured results by attacker tier, dated [X]." |

Admin documentation should say plainly that the operator is the controller and needs a record per form with two parts: the Art. 5(3) or § 25 exemption analysis and a separate legitimate-interest assessment, since German guidance treats the second as unable to stand in for the first. It should generate privacy notice text from the actual configuration, prompt a DPIA assessment when behavioural signals are on, flag CDNs, WAFs and managed Redis as processors, and warn that reusing security data for analytics destroys the exemption. Retention numbers are engineering judgement, since no regulator specifies any: raw signals discarded after evaluation, per-site keys rotated about every 24 hours, and token state kept only until expiry. Fingerprint-gated forms should stay off legally mandated flows such as cancellation buttons without an alternative ([IT-Recht Kanzlei](https://www.it-recht-kanzlei.de/captcha-kuendigungsbutton-widerrufsbutton.html), read via summary).

## Doable as a Docker stack if maintenance is treated as the product

### The stack is small, and the deployment topology sets signal quality

The minimal stack is an API and widget service, a Valkey or Redis store for challenge state and redemption (Cap's standalone uses [Valkey](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tiagozip/cap/main/docs/guide/workings.md)), and a sidecar refreshing local IP data. An optional compose profile runs the attacker and browser lab. Local IP context needs no external calls: [iptoasn](https://iptoasn.com/) is public domain with hourly updates, and cloud range files such as [AWS](https://ip-ranges.amazonaws.com/ip-ranges.json) and the [Tor exit list](https://check.torproject.org/torbulkexitlist) were downloadable when probed. DB-IP's free ASN file requires a backlink on pages using results ([DB-IP](https://db-ip.com/db/download/ip-to-asn-lite)), which is awkward in a widget. No free hosting-versus-residential ASN classification was verified, so the project would maintain one, and residential proxies bypass it regardless.

Signal fidelity depends on who terminates TLS (ranking is my judgement). Best is a dedicated `captcha.example.com` terminated by the service itself or reached by layer-4 passthrough. FCaptcha's release notes show this works natively in Go 1.24, and they also admit a Chrome fingerprint was mislabelled, which shows how easy mistakes are ([FCaptcha](https://github.com/WebDecoy/FCaptcha/releases/tag/v1.19.0)). Next is a sidecar like [fingerproxy](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/wi1dcard/fingerproxy/master/README.md) (Apache-2.0, forwards JA3, JA4 and HTTP/2 headers). A proxy module gives partial data through a header accepted only from trusted IPs. With a CDN in front, the origin sees the CDN's handshake. A plain layer-7 proxy gives nothing, which must report "unavailable" rather than suspicious. Cloudflare skips fingerprints on resumed sessions ([Cloudflare](https://developers.cloudflare.com/bots/additional-configurations/ja3-ja4-fingerprint/)). Keeping HTTP/3 off this hostname and capturing fingerprints per connection avoids further gaps.

### Mirror provider paths, and translate at each route's edge

The working pattern in reviewed products is base-URL substitution with mirrored paths and JavaScript globals, and [FCaptcha's README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WebDecoy/FCaptcha/main/README.md) describes the fullest precedent, which is the source of the path map below; its conformance is untested. Keep one internal risk value and convert it per route, because **hCaptcha's score runs in the opposite direction to reCAPTCHA's** ([hCaptcha](https://docs.hcaptcha.com/switch)).

| | reCAPTCHA v2/v3 | hCaptcha | Turnstile |
|---|---|---|---|
| Script path | `/recaptcha/api.js` | `/1/api.js` | `/turnstile/v0/api.js` |
| Verify path | `/recaptcha/api/siteverify` | `/siteverify` | `/turnstile/v0/siteverify` |
| Form field | `g-recaptcha-response` | `h-captcha-response` (FCaptcha also fills `g-recaptcha-response`) | `cf-turnstile-response` |
| Global | `grecaptcha` | `hcaptcha` | `turnstile` |
| Token life | [2 min](https://developers.google.com/recaptcha/docs/v3) | [120 s default](https://docs.hcaptcha.com/) | [300 s](https://developers.cloudflare.com/turnstile/get-started/server-side-validation/) |
| Replay error | `timeout-or-duplicate` | `already-seen-response` | `timeout-or-duplicate` |
| Score | 1.0 = human (v3) | 1.0 = threat (Enterprise) | None on free plan |

Start with Turnstile's verify contract, which is the most explicit and accepts form and JSON bodies, then add reCAPTCHA, whose `execute()` must branch on argument type: a string site key means v3, while a widget ID or nothing means v2 ([v3](https://developers.google.com/recaptcha/docs/v3), [invisible](https://developers.google.com/recaptcha/docs/invisible)). hCaptcha follows. Friendly Captcha has a different shape in both versions and is lower priority, and its v1 docs advise accepting forms when verify returns non-200, which would fail open during a compat-server outage ([v1](https://developer.friendlycaptcha.com/docs/v1/api/)). `grecaptcha.enterprise` and the assessment API were not researched, and they matter because classic keys were due to move to Google Cloud ([migration](https://cloud.google.com/recaptcha/docs/migration-overview?hl=zh-cn)).

Mandatory binding has a compatibility catch. The compat protocols do not let callers send an expected action, and hCaptcha alone adds an optional `sitekey` parameter. Many integrators skip these checks anyway. The server should therefore enforce allowed hostnames and actions from per-site configuration at verify time, which improves on the hosted products rather than simply copying them.

Reach is uneven. Gitea (`RECAPTCHA_URL`), the Ruby gem and `google/recaptcha` accept custom URLs, and `django-recaptcha` accepts a domain, so mirroring paths covers it ([Gitea](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/go-gitea/gitea/main/custom/conf/app.example.ini), [Ruby gem](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ambethia/recaptcha/master/README.md), [PHP](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google/recaptcha/master/src/ReCaptcha/RequestMethod/CurlPost.php), [django-recaptcha](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/django-recaptcha/django-recaptcha/main/README.md)). **Keycloak, Contact Form 7 and `react-google-recaptcha` offer only a switch to `recaptcha.net`**, so they need code changes or plugins ([Keycloak](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/keycloak/keycloak/main/services/src/main/java/org/keycloak/authentication/forms/AbstractRegistrationRecaptcha.java), [CF7](https://plugins.svn.wordpress.org/contact-form-7/trunk/modules/recaptcha/service.php), [react-google-recaptcha](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/dozoisch/react-google-recaptcha/master/README.md)). DNS overrides are not a general route, since browsers would need certificates for provider hostnames that no public CA will issue you; I found no production example. Gitea's native `MCAPTCHA_URL` suggests a better long-term path: being accepted as a named provider. Trademark or terms risk from mirroring paths was not researched, and the widget UI should carry no provider branding.

### Build order and go/no-go test

My priority order, unmeasured since no public data shows each layer's marginal value, is: protocol correctness and token binding; the response ladder with shadow mode; consistency rules shipped as updatable lists; local velocity limits; the attacker lab; PoW tuned on low-end phones; the accessible alternative; behavioural scoring; and visual challenges last. Strict mode should ship from day one because it is the simplest path and needs the least data.

The go/no-go test is cheap. Before writing much code, stand up FCaptcha and Cap and run the lab against them, with Chrome, Firefox, Safari, Brave and Tor or Mullvad as negative controls, and Playwright, Puppeteer, Selenium, Patchright, rebrowser, Camoufox and default agents as positive ones. Add disabled-user testing on the input modes above. This produces the independent efficacy data the field lacks, and it settles whether to fork or start fresh.

The limits to publish are these. A self-hoster's detection will trail global vendors on first visits, and low-traffic sites get weak learning. Public code means public features, so every client check decays once tools tune against it, and this requires ongoing maintenance of rules and harness. Solver services and self-hosted solver projects are multiplying ([GitHub search](https://api.github.com/search/repositories?q=captcha+self-hosted+created:%3E2025-01-01&sort=stars&per_page=15)). Privacy tools, VPNs, old devices and assistive setups will generate false positives, so operators need measured rates by segment and a route for appeal. Results should be reported by attacker tier and dated, never as one number.

## Conclusion

The research shifts the project's definition of success from building a Turnstile clone to building an auditable cost-and-evidence system. Detection features are the wrong differentiator: FCaptcha already claims most of that list under MIT, with no independent evidence either way. The open position is **verifiable honesty**, meaning a mode whose privacy guarantees an admin can test, a signal register an operator can copy into a privacy notice, and dated efficacy figures by attacker tier from a public lab. FCaptcha publishes a benchmark harness with false-positive budgets, which comes closest, but none of the products reviewed documents the full combination. The same lab doubles as the training source that per-site calibration needs.

The durable bets are not the most impressive current numbers. Today's best agent signal, the teleporting click, is one library import away from disappearing, and the most agent-resistant puzzles conflict with accessibility duties. What should last is the architecture: single-use tokens that charge per action, server-held decisions, cross-layer contradictions that make every lie expensive, and escalation rather than denial. The most important unknowns are measurable by this project itself: false positives among disabled users, the real lift of each layer on small sites, and how quickly agents adapt. Regulation may ease under the Omnibus, but the Austrian reasoning and the conflicting CNIL signals mean the sound plan is to treat a consent-free legal position as a documented risk decision by each operator, not as a feature the software can deliver.
